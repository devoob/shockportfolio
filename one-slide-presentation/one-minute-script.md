## The script

> **Hello, I'm Jonathan Devano. This is Shock Lens. Anna is a self-directed
> investor: a $100,000 portfolio, fifteen holdings. Headlines tell her what
> happened, never what it costs her — so she guesses.**
>
> Prediction markets have traded over three million dollars on the AI bubble
> — a seven percent chance this year — yet nothing turns that into her
> number.
>
> **Shock Lens does:** pick any scenario and see the estimated loss on her
> holdings — $9,690 for the AI bubble — and which ones drive it. AI
> interprets; code calculates; auditable.
>
> Anna pays nothing; her broker pays per client, because informed clients
> panic less and leave less.
>
> One integration reaches every client; scenarios are data files, not
> builds; marginal cost is AI compute.
>
> **Next: pilot one broker, test retention. Today, Anna guesses. With Shock
> Lens, she sees it first. Thank you.**

## Sentence-by-sentence reference mapping

| Script beat | Reference "Outstanding" move | Why it earns marks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "Anna is a self-directed investor: a $100,000 portfolio, fifteen holdings." | "For professionals handling email all day…" — a specific customer situation, not "everyone". | Pain (30): a concrete customer the listener can picture, with stakes named in the same breath. |
| "Headlines tell her what happened, never what it costs her — so she guesses." | "repeated clicking… consume attention needed for their main work" — the recurring difficulty, stated plainly. | Pain (30): names the exact gap and the bad behaviour it produces (guessing), not a vague dislike of news. |
| "Prediction markets have traded over three million dollars on the AI bubble — a seven percent chance this year — yet nothing turns that into her number." | "One user has paid US$30 monthly for three years…" — behavioural evidence, used proportionately. | Pain (30): real money is already at stake on this risk ($3.2M volume, dated on the slide), and the gap is specific: nobody converts it per portfolio. |
| "Shock Lens does: pick any scenario and see the estimated loss on her holdings — $9,690 for the AI bubble — and which ones drive it." | "ClearMail would combine keyboard shortcuts, AI-assisted replies and scheduling…" — what the product actually is, concretely. | Solution (30): product defined by what it does, with a real output number, not by category ("an app"). |
| "AI interprets; code calculates; auditable." | "Our brand would emphasise dependable productivity" — the distinctive choice. | Solution (30): the differentiation — headline risk tools and robo-advisors are black boxes; our numbers are reproducible and every assumption is labelled. |
| "Anna pays nothing; her broker pays per client, because informed clients panic less and leave less." | "We would charge US$30 monthly, targeting people who value… to pay despite free alternatives." — payer, and why they pay. | Solution (30): the payer is identified (broker), free for the user, and the reason is an economic one the broker already cares about: retention. |
| "One integration reaches every client; scenarios are data files, not builds; marginal cost is AI compute." | "Shared integrations and standard onboarding could support more subscribers without custom builds." — reach + repeatable delivery. | Scale (20): one sale reaches an entire platform; new products are files, not engineering; the cost per added user stays small. |
| "Next: pilot one broker, test retention." | "We would test time saved, renewal, and AI and support costs…" — a concrete first test. | Scale (20): shows how the claim gets validated, which the outstanding example makes explicit; turns the business case into a plan. |
| "Today, Anna guesses. With Shock Lens, she sees it first." | (Closing synthesis.) | Presentation (20): one clean loop back to the opening; finishes inside 60 s. |
