# IIMT1611 Individual Pitch Session — Thursday, 8 October 2026

Each student will deliver a **one-minute individual pitch** proposing a
technology-based innovation. This assessment counts towards **15% of your
course grade**. You may use **one slide**.

Your pitch should explain:

1. **The pain point:** Who has the problem, why does it matter, and what
   suggests it is real?
2. **The commercial answer:** What is your solution, how does it address
   the problem, and who would pay for it and why?
3. **Scalability:** Why could this become a business serving substantially
   more customers, and how could that expansion work?

The session will also help you hear your classmates' ideas and identify
potential group-project partners.

## Assessment rubric

| Criterion | Marks | What we are looking for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Customer pain and value** | **30** | A specific customer, a meaningful problem, and relevant evidence explaining why it matters. |
| **Commercial solution and business logic** | **30** | A clear technology-based solution, a convincing connection to the problem, an identifiable payer and reason for payment, and useful differentiation from alternatives. |
| **Scalability and growth potential** | **20** | A credible explanation of how the business could reach and serve more customers with manageable costs and operational demands. |
| **Presentation** | **20** | A clear, coherent and understandable pitch that uses the available time effectively. Any slide should support the message. |

Support your argument with relevant evidence, such as a customer
observation, a published statistic or a comparison with an existing
solution. Clearly distinguish **what you know from what you are
assuming**.

Saying that your market is large or that your solution is an app does
not, by itself, explain scalability.

Both instructors will assess each student independently. Individual
feedback will be provided privately.

## Arrangements for the session

- **Arrive on time and attend the whole session.** You need to hear
  everyone's pitch when considering potential groupmates; you are welcome
  to talk to your favoured potential partners after the pitch session.
- Pitching order will be determined by a **public lucky draw** during the
  session.
- Begin by stating your name and the title of your idea.
- Aim to finish within **60 seconds**. You will receive a **15-second
  buffer**, but will be asked to stop at **75 seconds**.
- Keep notes on classmates whose ideas interest you.

We will open an **online platform for you to register your top choices of
group-project partners**. The link and instructions will be shared
separately.

Your final groupmates will be determined through **Prof. Li's matching
algorithm**, taking into account **your choices, other students' choices,
and performance in this individual pitch**. Stating a preference does not
guarantee a particular match.
